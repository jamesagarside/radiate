# Design standards

These are the rules for Radiate's web UI (`web/`). The same UI runs standalone on port 8099, in the Home Assistant sidebar through ingress, and in the demo. It is a dashboard people glance at many times a day, so it should be quick to read, calm, and say in plain words what the heating is doing.

The look is called "room table". The House page is a line of readings, the drawing of the house with a floor picker, and a real table of rooms beside it, where a room's row opens in place to change it. Type is Overpass on a fluid scale. Colours are Radix steps: slate for the page, and colour only for temperature or for a state that also has words. Controls are black and white.

The owner turned down these looks, so don't bring them back:

- Rounded cards with shadows, the usual card kit.
- Cream or concrete backgrounds, and plates with a cut corner.
- Uppercase eyebrow labels over sections.
- Geist, or any other fashionable grotesk set heavy on every number.
- Glassy indigo or magenta gradients.
- A big number with a small label as a hero, and rows of stat tiles.
- Stacked banners with a thick coloured rule down the left.

The values on this page are the ones in `web/app.css`, `web/house.css`, `web/guide.css`, `web/savings.css`, `web/settings.css` and `web/data.css` today. Change the CSS and this page together.

## Principles

- **Explain the house.** Every room says, in words, why Radiate is doing what it does.
- **Label and value.** Facts read as a label then a value ("Boiler On, 16 min"), in a line or a table, never as tiles.
- **Colour means temperature.** The temperature scale colours rooms. Other colours only back up a word (a state, an alert). Buttons, segmented controls and links are ink.
- **Little motion.** Motion only where it explains a state change, short, and none under reduced motion beyond fades.
- **Good defaults over options.** No theme picker or layout settings. Light and dark follow the system.
- **Get the small details right.** Tabular numbers that don't jiggle, press feedback on every button, and hover effects that don't fire on touch.
- **Update in place.** Elements are created once and updated, so focus survives each tick and transitions retarget instead of restarting.

## Type

One family, Overpass, served with the UI from `web/fonts` (Latin subsets of 400, 600 and 800 from Fontsource, SIL Open Font License 1.1). The page makes no requests outside the Radiate server. `index.html` preloads 400 and 800. `--font` names it; `--body`, `--display` and `--readout` are kept as aliases of it.

Sizes are a Utopia fluid scale, ratio 1.2 at 320 px to 1.25 at 1240 px, so there are no type breakpoints:

| Token | Size | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `--step--1` | `clamp(0.83rem, 0.81rem + 0.1vw, 0.9rem)` | Labels, table heads, states, help lines, buttons, chips, the status line |
| `--step-0` | `clamp(1rem, 0.97rem + 0.16vw, 1.125rem)` | Body text, the readings, table cells |
| `--step-1` | `clamp(1.2rem, 1.14rem + 0.29vw, 1.41rem)` | Section headings, the wordmark, a room's temperature in the table, the stepper value, figures |
| `--step-2` | `clamp(1.44rem, 1.34rem + 0.49vw, 1.76rem)` | View headings (Devices, Settings, Guide) and the sign-in heading |
| `--step-3` | `clamp(1.73rem, 1.57rem + 0.78vw, 2.2rem)` | Not used yet |

- Weights: 400 for text, 600 for labels, controls, values in the readings and room names, 800 for headings, the wordmark and the numbers you read first (a room's temperature, the stepper, figures).
- Line height 1.45 for text, 1.2 for headings.
- Every number uses `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`, so live values don't shift sideways.
- Sentence case everywhere, headings included. No uppercase labels.
- The wordmark is "Radiate", 800, with the four-bar mark in temperature colours.

## Motion

Motion follows Emil Kowalski's rules, and there is little of it.

| Rule | How it is done |
|---|---|
| Animate `transform` and `opacity` only | Valve bars and the burn guard scale with `transform: scaleX()` from the left |
| Strong ease-out | `--ease-out: cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1)`. Never `ease-in` |
| Short | Press feedback 160 ms, bars 240 ms, colour changes 150 ms with plain `ease` |
| Press feedback | `scale(0.97)` on `:active` for buttons, segments and links; `scale(0.95)` for the stepper buttons |
| Hover only for real pointers | Inside `@media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`, such as the room row's tint |
| Respect reduced motion | Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, movement and press scaling stop; opacity and colour fades stay |
| No animation on keyboard-driven changes | Keyboard actions apply instantly |
| Name exact properties | Never `transition: all` |
| Transitions, not keyframes, for live data | Values change every tick; transitions retarget |

A room's row opens in place with nothing moving. New UI that enters (the account menu) starts from `scale(0.95)` and `opacity: 0`; the sign-in card from `scale(0.98)` with `@starting-style`. Stale data fades to `opacity: 0.55` over 200 ms. Selecting a room in the drawing scrolls its row into view smoothly, or jumps under reduced motion.

The drawing has the page's one keyframe animation. A room's radiator bar blinks while the room takes heat, and always on a room with a hand valve: a hard on and off (`steps(2)`) every 1.8 s, with no fade and no movement. It stops under reduced motion and the bar stays on. The household setup's steps fade in from `opacity: 0` and `translateY(6px)` over 200 ms, except when moved by the keyboard.

## Accessibility

- The page declares `lang="en-GB"` and `color-scheme: light dark`.
- Text meets WCAG 2.1 AA contrast: 4.5:1 for body and small text, 3:1 for large text. Check both themes when you add or change a token.
- Every interactive element is a real `<button>` or link with a visible label. Toggles use `aria-pressed`, and the Fire lit button shows the fire switch a person set (`fire_switch`), not a detected fire. Heating, Burns and the floor picker are each a `radiogroup` of `role="radio"` buttons with `aria-checked`, labelled by their visible label; Burns is described by its note.
- The focus ring is `outline: 2px solid var(--focus)` with a 2px offset on `:focus-visible` (-3px inside a segmented control). Never remove it. A room in the drawing shows focus as a 5px `--focus` outline round its top.
- The status line is `aria-live="polite"`.
- The room table is a real `table` with column headers and a `th scope="row"` per room. Floors are `th scope="rowgroup"`. Each room's name is a button with `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls` pointing at its open row. The state appears twice in the markup (under the name on a phone, in its own column on a wide screen); the hidden one is `display: none`, so it is read once.
- Decorative graphics (the brand mark, the swatches, the valve bars) are `aria-hidden="true"`.
- Touch targets are at least 44 px on touch screens. The stepper buttons are 44 by 44 px everywhere. Under `(pointer: coarse)`, buttons, segments, tabs and inputs get `min-height: 44px`, and text links get 13 px of extra hit area above and below.
- In the drawing, each room is a focusable `role="button"` with `aria-pressed` and a label such as "Lounge, 20.5 degrees, heating". Enter or Space selects it and opens its row.
- The stepper is a `role="group"` labelled "Set Lounge". Each button has its own label ("Raise Lounge by 0.5°") and the value is `aria-live="polite"`. A button is disabled at 5° or 28°.
- The household setup, the week editor, Settings and the login page keep the patterns described under their components.

Stale data is the one deliberate exception to the contrast rule: it dims to 55% so it reads as out of date, and the status line above it keeps full contrast and says how old it is.

## Copy

UI text follows the docs register: UK English, metric units, no em or en dashes as punctuation, sentence case, plain words, written the GOV.UK way. Short and terse in the table, like a well-written appliance manual; full sentences in reasons, notes and the Guide.

- **Point first.** The state, then the detail: "Burner on for 12 min. Heating Lounge and Bedroom 3."
- **Label and value.** "Boiler On, 16 min", "Rooms heating 7 of 9", "Then 16.0° at 08:30".
- **Plain words over internal terms.** "Setting" or "Set", not setpoint. "Warming early", not preheating. "Manual valve", not bypass. "Fewer, longer" and "Sooner", not Eco and Comfort (the Guide says what the house file and Home Assistant call them). "Taking spare heat", not heat sink. "Shortest burn", not minimum burn.
- **No arrows standing in for words.**
- **No repeated prefixes.** A reason in a room's row drops the room's name the core starts it with.
- **Learning in sentences**: "Paused while the fire is lit", "Paused: a window may be open", "Settling after a change".
- **Headings say what is there**: "House", "Rooms", "Today", "Boiler", "Schedules". No headings with personality.
- **Help lines sparingly.** One per view or panel at most, with one link into the Guide that names the topic ("Reading the house"), never "Learn more".
- **Units every time**: `20.5°`, `35%`, `12 min`. Times are 24-hour (`07:10`). A whole degree in a sentence drops ".0" ("to 16°").
- **Missing values** show `–`, never `0` or blank. This is the only dash character in the UI.
- **Errors say what happened and what Radiate is doing about it**: "Cannot reach Radiate. Retrying… Showing data from 14:32."
- No exclamation marks, no blame, no marketing words.

## States to design for

Every view has to make sense in each of these states. When you add a component, decide what it shows in each one.

| State | What the reader must see | How it shows today |
|---|---|---|
| Loading | That the page is connecting | Status line "Connecting…" until the first state arrives |
| Offline | That the data is stale and Radiate is retrying | When the WebSocket drops, the page polls once per reconnect attempt (2 s, backing off to 15 s). If the poll fails too, the page dims and the status line, in amber, says "Cannot reach Radiate. Retrying… Showing data from 14:32." The next state from either path clears it |
| Demo | That nothing is real | A white band with a hairline round it, with the simulated time and the boiler water, hot-water store and stairs temperatures |
| Dry run | That Radiate is not writing to devices | Persistent white band under the header with an amber "Dry run" tag, the held-back valve writes and boiler commands, and the latest five writes behind a disclosure |
| Alerts | What needs attention, in words | "Needs attention" panel, heading in amber, under the readings, one alert per list item. A problem shared by several rooms is one line, for example "TRV not reachable (8 rooms): Bedroom 1, …". After four lines the rest sit behind "Show N more" |
| Sign-in | That the server needs its token | When the API answers 401 and login is off, a sign-in card replaces the page. The token is kept in `localStorage` and sent as a Bearer header and as the stream's first message. Never shown through Home Assistant ingress or in the demo |
| Login | Who can sign in, and how | With login on (`GET auth/config`), a 401 sends the page to `login` instead of the token card. See the login page component. Never shown through Home Assistant ingress, to token users or in the demo |
| Signed in | Who is signed in, their role, and how to sign out | The account menu in the header (see its component), shown only for a login session |
| Role | What this person can't change, and why | A control the role can't use stays in place, disabled, with a neutral tag beside it (`.lock-tag`, `--muted` on `--surface-2`, 0.72rem 600): "Read only" for a viewer, "Ask an admin" for a household member. A household member: Arrange rooms, Set up your household and Change household setup, Scan network. The Settings tab shows only for an admin. Locked controls have `cursor: not-allowed`, no press scale and no hover. The server refuses the same changes (403), so the tag is never the only check |
| Safe mode | That the boiler is stopping and why | Shown as an alert |
| Change on its way | That the press counted, and whether the server took it | A press on the heating mode, demand mode, Boost hot water or a Fire lit button shows its new state at once (`web/pending.js`), with `aria-busy` and the control at 0.75 opacity while the request is out; the stepper shows its new value the same way. Straight after the server answers, the page fetches the state, so the change doesn't wait for the next tick. A state from before the press can't undo it: the press holds until a state agrees, or 5 s after the server took it. A refused change goes back to the server's value with a line in amber under the control (`role="alert"`): "Not saved: ask an admin." for a role refusal, "Not saved: your sign-in has expired. Reload the page." for a stale session, otherwise "Not saved. Try again." Back now and Save disable while they send |
| Boiler states | Off, opening valves, on, resting, with shortest burn and rest progress, and why the burner is on | Boiler reading and chip ("Off", "Opening valves", "On", "Resting"), status line and guard bar. The guard bar and countdown use the effective minimum off from `state` |
| Boiler not linked | That Radiate can't switch the boiler, so it never claims the boiler is idle | When `telemetry.boiler_linked` is false (no `shelly` section), the status line reads "No room needs heat. The boiler switch isn't linked yet." or "Lounge and Toilet want heat. The boiler switch isn't linked yet, so the boiler follows its own timer." The Boiler reading says "Not linked". Servers that don't send `boiler_linked`, and the demo, keep the usual wording |
| House layout | Whether the drawing shows the owner's layout or a guess | Until a layout is saved, a muted line under the key: "Rooms are placed by a guess from their names. Arrange them to match your house." A server without `GET layout` keeps the guess, and the editor says "This server can't keep a layout. Update Radiate to save it." A refused save reads "Not saved: …" with the server's message; a failed one, "Not saved. Check the connection and move a room to try again." |
| Short-cycling | That minimum off has been raised and for how long | Line under the guard bar with an amber "Short-cycling" tag: "Minimum off raised to 15 min for the next 23 h 30 min, so the burner starts less often." Hidden when `boiler.short_cycle` is absent or null |

## Components

**Readings.** The first thing on the House page (`web/house.js`, `.readings`): one line of facts, each a muted label and a 600-weight value, wrapping on a phone. Boiler ("On, 16 min" in `--heat`, "Off", "Opening valves", "Resting" or "Not linked"), Hot water ("On", "On, boosted", "Off"), Rooms heating ("7 of 9", or Heating "Off" or "Frost only"), Quiet hours ("until 06:00") while they hold, Outside, Next change ("08:30, Bedroom 1 and Bedroom 2 to 16°") and, when any need it, Devices "3 need attention" as an amber link to `#devices`. It is rewritten only when its words change, so focus on the link survives a tick. The line at the top of every page still says why the burner is on.

**House.** Left of the room table above 1000 px, and sticky there; above it below that. A `section-h` "House" with the floor picker beside it, the drawing, then the key ("12° [ramp] 22°", "Bar on a room: radiator on." and a "Reading the house" link), the guessed-layout line and "Arrange rooms". The drawing is an axonometric model: each floor's rooms as blocks, floors stacked and pulled apart so an upper floor never hides the one below, each floor tagged with its name. A block's top is the room's temperature colour and its two sides are darker shades of it. The reading sits on the top in 800, with the name under it when the room is at least 1.4 m wide. A room with no reading is `--t-none` and reads `–`. A radiator bar runs along the front edge while the room takes heat, and always on a room with a hand valve (see Motion). Selecting a room outlines its top in ink and opens its row in the table; selecting it again closes it. The drawing is redrawn on each state, when the colour scheme changes and once the fonts load. The layout only draws the house and groups the table; control never reads it.

**Floor picker.** A segmented `radiogroup` beside the House heading, shown with more than one floor: "Both floors" (or "All floors" for three or more), then each floor by name, top floor first. Choosing a floor draws it alone at ground level, so it fills the space; that helps most on a phone. Arrow keys move and choose. Selecting a room on another floor, from the table, goes back to every floor.

**Room table.** Beside the house (`#rooms-table`, built by `web/app.js`), headed "Rooms" with "Select a room to change it." A real table: Room, Now, Set, Valve and State columns, one `tbody` per floor with the floor's name as a `rowgroup` header, top floor first, and rooms not on the plan last. There are no floor rows with a single floor. Each row: a 12 px swatch in the room's temperature colour, the name as an underlined button, the temperature in 800 at `--step-1`, the setting (indigo and bold while held), the opening limit as a 44 by 6 px `--heat` bar scaled with `transform` and its percentage (faded to 0.4 while the room isn't taking heat), and the state. On a wide screen the row tints `--surface-2` on hover and the whole row opens it. Rows are created once and moved only when the grouping changes, so focus survives.

**Room states.** The State column, and under the name below 600 px: "Heating", "Warming early" and "Warming ahead" on `--heat-soft`; "Waiting for a burn" and "Fire lit" on `--warn-soft`; "Holding" on `--ok-soft`; "Taking spare heat" on `--cool-soft`; "Waiting", "Manual valve" and "Unheated" neutral. Text on a tint is the step 12 ink.

**Open row.** Selecting a room opens a second row under it, `--surface-2`, with nothing moving. One row is open at a time. On the left: the stuck or leaking valve line on `--warn-soft` when it applies, then facts as a label and value list (Aiming for, when the target differs from the setting; Then "16.0° at 08:30"; Valve "Opens up to 70%" or "Closed"; Humidity; Learning "Yes" or why not), the reason in muted text, Fire lit for a room with an open fire, and "What Radiate has learned". On the right, for a controllable room, the setting: "Set Lounge", the stepper (minus, value, plus) in steps of 0.5 °C from 5 to 28 °C, and under it "From the schedule", or the hold ("Held until 22:30", "Held for 1 h 20 min", "Held until cleared") with "Resume schedule". Presses change the value at once and are sent together 600 ms after the last one; the value stays as pressed until a state confirms it, or for 5 s. A hold ends at the next scheduled change by default. Reasons from the core lose their leading room name. A passive room reads "Hand valve, always open. Keeps water moving round the system. Not controlled."; an unheated room "No radiator. Temperature recorded only."; otherwise, without a reason, "1.6° below setting." or "At or above setting."

**Today.** Under the house and the table, hidden until `GET schedules` has answered. A "Today" heading with a "Week schedules" link, one help line, then one row per scheduled room: the name, then today as a 16px strip of its settings in temperature colours, with an ink now line haloed in `--surface`. Hour ticks at 00, 06, 12, 18 and 24. Each strip is labelled with the day in words.

**Arrange rooms.** The house becomes a floor-plan editor, headed "Arrange rooms", with "Start again" and a primary "Done". Under the heading: one tab per floor (a segmented row, the selected one in ink), "Add floor" up to six floors, a "Floor name" field (up to 40 characters), and "Remove this floor" when a floor has no rooms and isn't the only one. The plan is a 14 by 10 m `--surface-2` well with a gridline every half metre and a stronger one every metre. Rooms are blocks in their temperature colour, with the name at the top and the size ("4 × 3.5 m") at the bottom. Dragging a room moves it, and dragging the 14 px ink square in its corner resizes it, using pointer events with `touch-action: none` on the plan. Everything snaps to 0.5 m and a side is at least 1 m. With the keyboard, Enter or Space selects a room, arrow keys move it half a metre, and Shift with an arrow resizes it. Under the plan, a tray lists rooms not placed yet as "Place Kitchen" chips (dashed outline), or says "Every room is placed.", and offers "Take Kitchen off the plan" for the selected room. A placed room goes in the first free 2 by 2 m spot on the floor. "Start again" puts every room back where the guess from their names puts them. Each change is saved with `PUT layout` 500 ms after the last one, and a muted line says "Saving…", then "Saved." or why not. While the editor is open, the room table steps aside for "How it will look": the drawing as it will be, with "2 floors, 8 of 9 rooms placed." and a sentence saying the layout only draws the house and groups the rooms. "Done" closes the editor and returns focus to "Arrange rooms".

**Guessed layout.** Until the owner saves a layout, rooms whose names suggest an upper floor (bedroom, bathroom, landing, en suite, nursery, study, loft, attic) go on "First floor" and the rest on "Ground floor", three to a row at 3 by 3 m. If every room matches, or none does, there is one floor.

**What Radiate has learned.** A `details` disclosure at the foot of a room's open row, for a room with a model, closed by default, over a 1px `--line` rule. The summary is "What Radiate has learned" (600) with the settings disclosure's chevron, which rotates over 160 ms (no transition under reduced motion), and a "Still learning" tag (`--cool` on `--cool-soft`) while the heating gain's confidence is under 0.4 or nothing is learned. The body appears in place with nothing moving: a "Still learning." line in `--cool` when the tag shows, then rows of a muted `--step--1` label over a sentence, split by `--line` rules, written by `web/learned.js` from `rooms[].model`: Warms up ("With the boiler on and the valve open, about 1.9° an hour while most of the house is heating, or 3.1° an hour when it's the only room taking heat. Now, with 7.2° outside, it would gain about 1.5° an hour after the heat it loses."), Cools down (for 10° between inside and outside, then now), Other heat (a range cooker, or steady heat of 0.15° an hour or more), Heat arrives (the arrival lag and how many warm-ups timed it, or "Not timed yet"), How sure (the heating gain's and the loss rate's confidence in words: "still learning" under 0.4, "fairly sure of" under 0.75, "sure of" above; readings, the date learning began and hours of learning; a valve-only room and a model learned only in dry run say so), Used for (optimum start with this room's next warm-up and the time it starts, or, while quiet hours hold the room, that they hold it until they end and it may arrive late, and the lockshield cap between burns), Relearned (change detection) and Started again (this room's learning notes). Under the rows, the model's own numbers in muted `--step--1` ("In the model's own terms: heating gain 3.81 K/h, …") and a help link, "What the room model is". The rows are rebuilt only when their words change and hold nothing focusable, so focus on the summary or the link survives each tick. It has no controls, so every role sees the same. No chart: the sentences carry the numbers, and a warm-up curve would only redraw the optimum start sum as a straight line. The summary is 44 px tall on touch screens.

**Person card.** Name and badge, one line of state, then "Set away" (or "Change") and a "Back now" link while an away until is set. The form appears in place, with no animation, and takes focus; Escape or "Cancel" closes it and returns focus to the button. Days read "today", "tomorrow", a weekday within the week, or "Tue 2 Dec"; a time of 00:00 is left out. Times are house time, taken from the server's local ISO strings.

**Week card.** Name and a neutral badge, "Household" or "Hand-written"; the away line and usual week when they apply; seven day rows (Mon to Sun) of a 24-hour bar; hour ticks at 00:00, 06:00, 12:00, 18:00 and 24:00; the week in words, one line per run of identical days; dropped-period lines; "Depends on …" with the room's people. A warm period is a block with a `--heat` edge and a `--heat` fill whose opacity follows the temperature (0.3 to 1 across the coolest and warmest setpoint shown, widened to the household levels), so 19° looks the same in every room. Its temperature is written above it in `--ember`; labels never overlap, and one that cannot fit is left to the day's words. Each bar is `role="img"` labelled with the day in words, and today's row has `aria-current="date"`, a bold day name and a `--text` now marker with a `--surface` halo. Faint `--line` gridlines every 3 hours. A legend lists the household levels with their temperatures, and "Adjusted" when a room has adjustments. The bar compresses on a phone. The pure helpers in `web/week.js` take minutes of the day, so the wizard's Result step reuses them; there the bars are read-only.

**Adjusting a week.** In the Schedules section, a household room's warm periods have a grip at each edge: a 6 px `--surface` bar with a `--heat` outline, `cursor: ew-resize`. With a mouse the grips fade in (opacity, 150 ms) while the pointer is over the card; on touch screens they are always shown. The hit area is 24 px wide, and 44 by 50 px under `(pointer: coarse)`. A run of identical days moves together, so dragging Wednesday's evening end moves Monday to Friday, and the change applies to those days. Dragging uses pointer events with pointer capture, one pointer at a time, `touch-action: none` on the grip only, and snaps to 15 minutes. While an edge moves, its time is written above it in bold `--text` and the day's temperature labels hide. Nothing animates: the block follows the pointer or key exactly. A later end or an earlier start keeps the room warm, the other way keeps it cold; an edge can't cross the other edge of its period, less 15 minutes, or a neighbouring period. On release the change is previewed (`POST household/preview`) and waits for Save. Keyboard: only the first day of each run is a tab stop. Each grip is `role="slider"` named "Bedroom 1, Mon to Fri, warm until 22:30", with the time as `aria-valuetext`. Arrow keys move it 15 minutes, Page Up and Page Down an hour, Home and End to its limits; the preview waits until the keys stop for 400 ms. Enter saves, Escape cancels and puts every edge back. After a save, focus returns to the same grip, or to "Adjust" after a pointer save. Adjusted time has a 3 px `--cool` line along the bottom of the bar. Under the week, a room with adjustments lists them in words ("Kept warm 22:30 to 23:00 on weekdays.") with a "Remove" link each, its week without them ("Without adjustments: …"), and "Reset to routine". "Adjust" opens a form in place with no animation: Days (every day, weekdays, weekend or a day), "Keep it" warm or cold, From and To as 24-hour text inputs. Escape or Cancel closes it and returns focus to "Adjust". An error appears under the fields in amber, and the field it is about gets `aria-invalid`. A room that someone being away has changed has no grips, as its week isn't its usual one, but keeps its list and the form.

**Setup wizard.** A heading with "Close", the five steps as a list (the current one bold with a `--cool` underline, visited ones as links), then one card per step: a heading, a one-line introduction, the questions, an "Advanced: …" disclosure, and Back, "Step 2 of 5: Routines, Sam" and Next along the bottom. Next reads "See the result" on Temperatures and "Save" on the Result. Questions are plain and name the person: "When does Sam get up?", "Is Sam out or working from home during the day?", "Where does Sam work?", "When does Sam go to bed?", "How is it used?", "What kind of room?", "Who uses it?". Every answer starts filled in, so Next always works on a first visit. On People, each person is a `role="group"` row with their name, "Home Assistant person" and Remove. "Home Assistant person" is a choice of "Not linked" and the people Home Assistant reported, by name (with the entity ID when two share a name), or, when Home Assistant hasn't reported any, a text input for the entity ID. One muted line under the list says what linking does, and every link's `aria-describedby` points to it. Each Advanced setting has a one-line explanation ending with its default ("Default 1 h."); per-person and per-room settings are empty, with the house's value as the placeholder. Room kinds are explained in a sentence under the choice. Below 12° an amber line under the unused or night temperature gives the damp note. Errors are amber, at the top of the step and under the answer. The Result is the week card for every room, with any advice and a sentence on warm periods left out of the valves' own schedules.

**Burns control.** The demand mode, labelled "Burns": a segmented control ("Fewer, longer" for `eco`, "Sooner" for `comfort`) after Heating, in the same style. Below 600 px the labels Heating and Burns hide, as the options name themselves. The control already names the mode, so the note under it says what the mode does. It starts "Default:" while the server follows the configuration file, and that prefix is the only sign of the default. Picking the configured default sends `null`, so Radiate follows the file again. A press shows at once and is held until a state confirms it, or for 5 s. The note ("Default: uses less oil. Rooms may warm a little later." or "Rooms warm sooner, with more burns.") sits right-aligned under the controls on wide screens.

**Device card.** Name ("Lounge TRV", "Bathroom room sensor", "Router plug_hall", "Boiler relay"; a room whose name ends in " room" doesn't repeat it, so "Dining room sensor") and a status badge: "Online" (`--ok`), "Needs attention" or "Offline" (amber), "No data yet" (neutral). Problem lines, then three values: Battery (the percentage and a 26 by 6 px meter scaled with `transform`, `--ok` or `--warn` when low; "Mains" for mains devices), Signal (three bars, `--ok` strong, `--muted` fair, `--warn` weak, the word, and "LQI 101" in muted 0.72rem under it; "Wi-Fi" for the relay) and Last seen ("just now", "4 min ago", "3 h ago"). Then the latest readings in one muted line ("18.1° at the valve, opening limit 35%, heating, auto mode", "20.4°, 56% humidity", "Heating on, hot water off"), and a meta line with the Zigbee2MQTT name in `code`, model, firmware and route ("Via plug_hall (LQI 93)", "Via the coordinator"). Cards sit in a `repeat(auto-fill, minmax(260px, 1fr))` grid under "Valves and sensors", "Zigbee network" (the scan card, the route tree and the routers) and "Boiler". The route tree is nested lists from the coordinator, each line a device with its link LQI, amber when weak. Cards are created once and updated in place; the meter is the only thing that moves.

**Suggestion card.** Title, point first and naming the room ("Bedroom 2 loses heat quickly", "Bedroom 3's radiator may be too small"), and a badge: "Act on this" (amber), "Worth considering" or "For information" (neutral). A card to act on also has a 3 px `--warn` left border, so the most pressing stand out without colour alone. Then the evidence in words, with the numbers and units ("cools by about 1.7° an hour. That is 3.3 times the house's typical 0.5° an hour."), a muted "What to do" label over the action, and a muted line with the confidence and what it rests on ("Medium confidence. From 87 h of learning in Bedroom 2."). Words that hedge ("may", "about", "Radiate's model says") are deliberate: a suggestion is never presented as fact. Cards sit in a `repeat(auto-fill, minmax(min(100%, 340px), 1fr))` grid, are created once and updated in place, and nothing on them moves.

**Savings chart.** Inline SVG drawn by `web/savings.js`, no chart library, redrawn only when the body or the width changes (a `ResizeObserver`), at 220px tall, 180px under 520px. One slot per day, or per week with the Days and Weeks segmented control (shown from 8 days). Oil used is a solid `--heat` bar; the baseline's litres a wider 1.5px `--text` outline drawn around it, so the gap above the bar is the saving and a day that used more shows its bar above the outline: outline against fill, never colour alone. A day not counted is a full-height slot hatched in `--line`, never a zero. The y axis starts at zero with a 1.5px ink zero line, `--line` gridlines at a round step (about four), and the top tick carries "L"; ticks and date labels are 800, 11px, tabular. The chart is a `role="group"` labelled with its trend ("Bar chart of oil used each day against the baseline, 24 days from 2 Nov to 25 Nov. Less than the baseline on 18, more on 5. 1 not counted."). Each slot is a `role="img"` with its words as `aria-label`; one slot is a tab stop and the arrow keys, Home and End move between them, with a 2px `--cool` ring on `:focus-visible`. The chosen slot's words sit in a line under the chart (`.sv-readout`), so they read the same with a mouse (hover), the keyboard and touch (tap), and it defaults to the latest day. A legend of swatches names "Oil used", "Baseline, estimated" and "Not counted". "Show as table" (`aria-pressed`) shows the same numbers, one row per day, in a table that scrolls inside its own box on a phone. Nothing in the chart animates, and numbers never count up.

**Savings side panels.** Beside the chart above 1100px, under it below. "Saved so far": a 90px line of the running total in ink against a zero line, and a sentence with the total. "Estimated share, last 30 days": one row per room, sorted, a 10px `--surface-2` track with an ink bar scaled with `transform` from zero, right for a saving and left of a centre ink line for oil over the baseline, the litres with a real minus sign, and the room's warm share and shortfall in muted 0.78rem text.

**Raw data view.** `#data`, tab "Raw data" between Savings and Guide, shown when `GET data/series` answers with a history (`web/data.js`, `web/data.css`). Moving to the view focuses its heading. The head says what is recorded ("65 series from Mon 16 Nov 05:00 to now. Kept for 14 days.") and has one help line linking to the Guide's "Raw data and exporting it". Above 1100 px a 360 px column holds "Find a reading" and the export panel, with the chart panel beside them; below that they stack: controls, chart, export. "Find a reading" has a search field whose matches are a list of plain buttons under it (Enter picks the first, Escape clears), then Reading and Room selects (Room is "Whole house" and disabled for a house reading), a Time segmented control (6 h, 24 h, 7 days, 30 days), From and To `datetime-local` fields in house time (editing one makes the range custom), "One point every", a muted line saying what the answer covers, and Download CSV and Download JSON. The chart panel: the series as its heading ("Lounge temperature", "Temperature, every room"), "Show as table", statistics in the readout face (Latest, Lowest, Highest, Average; Latest and the share of time On for a state), then the chart: inline SVG, 260 px tall, 200 px under 520 px, redrawn only when the answer or the width changes. One series is in 2px ink; with several, the rest are 1.25px `--muted` at 0.45 opacity, and a legend of `aria-pressed` buttons with a line swatch picks which is in ink, so colour never tells them apart, and a muted line says whose figures the statistics are. Over a step wider than a minute, the ink series' lowest to highest in each step is a `--surface-2` band. States and levels draw as steps; their axis reads Off and On, or 0% to 100% when aggregated, or the level names. A 1.5px ink baseline, `--line` gridlines, ticks in 800 at 11px. The chart is one tab stop: arrow keys, Page Up and Page Down, Home and End move a cobalt cursor with a dot, and the words for that point sit in a line under it (`aria-live`), for pointer and touch too. The table shows the newest 500 points in a box at most 480 px tall with a sticky header. The export panel: "OpenTelemetry export" with a badge, "Sending" (`--ok`), "Waiting" (cool), "Failing" (amber, and a 3px inset `--warn` rule on the panel) or "Not set up" or "Off" (neutral), one line point first ("Last sent at 09:28, just now.", "Can't send: HTTP 401: ... Trying again in 4 min, and keeping the data until then."), then where it sends, what is waiting, what was sent, and in amber what was dropped or rejected. Indexing is a white band with a hairline under the head; refusals and load errors are an amber band. Nothing animates beyond the shared press feedback.

**Learning list.** One row per controllable room in a card: the name, a 48 by 6 px `--cool` meter of hours learned against the hours needed (scaled with `transform`, only while learning), a badge "Ready" (`--ok`), "Learning" or "Not judged" (neutral), and the note in muted 0.84rem text.

**Settings view.** `#settings`, drawn by `web/settings.js` from the server's registry, so a new setting needs no UI code. The Guide's layout: a contents panel (two columns under 980 px) and one column of at most 52rem. Each section is an `h3.section-h` with a help line and one guide link, then a panel of rows. A row is the label, its source as a chip ("House file" and "Default" neutral, "Set here" `--cool` on `--cool-soft`, with a 3 px `--cool` inset rule down the row), an amber "Restart to use" tag while waiting, the help with its range, default and "Used after a restart." where that applies, and the control on the right (stacked under 640 px). Type to replace it"). A row set here ends with "House file: 8 min." and a link, "Use the house file's, 8 min" or "Use the default, 7°". Each section, and each room, is its own form: "Save changes" appears only once something changed, with "Cancel changes"; "Use the house file for this section" shows when anything in it is set here. Rooms are `details` panels, closed by default, with "2 set here" and "Restart to use" badges in the summary. A change that can write to the boiler or valves opens an amber band in place (`--warn-soft`, `--warn-ink`): "This lets Radiate write to the boiler or the valves: Dry run, Off. Check the wiring and the valves before you go on." with "Save and allow it" (focused) and "Cancel". The household and schedules sections have one button each: "Open the household setup" and "Show the schedules". "Use the house file for every setting" asks in place before it goes. Nothing animates except the room disclosure's chevron.

**Guide view.** `#guide`, static text in `index.html`, moved to by `web/guide.js`. A `--step-2` "Guide" heading and one line, then a contents panel (250 px, sticky at `top: 128px` above 980 px, in columns above the text below that) and one reading column of at most 40rem. Each section answers one question, in docs register: What the colours mean, Reading the house, What the boiler is doing, Reading the room table, Make a room warmer for a while, Change the weekly times, Hot water, Heating and burns, When someone is away, Open fires and range cookers, What the room model is, What learning is, Why a room isn't heating, Devices, Suggestions, Savings, Settings, The security log, Raw data and exporting it, Alerts, When something goes wrong, Home Assistant. A section is a `section` with an `h3.section-h` (`tabindex="-1"`, `scroll-margin-top: 136px`). Terms are a `dl` (`.g-terms`) of the state, chip or word exactly as the app shows it beside its meaning, one per row between `--line` hairlines; under 560 px the meaning drops under the term. Pictures (`.g-fig`, small white panels, `aria-hidden`, no pointer events) reuse what they explain: the temperature scale as a gradient of the `--t*` tokens, a block at 19° with its blinking radiator bar (`.hm-rad`), the valve bar and a held stepper. No images, icons or new fonts. Only behaviour the code has goes in, and what the app can't do is said plainly (hand-written weeks and hot-water windows live in the house file; this page's holds always end at the next change).

**Guide routing.** The view is the part of the hash before a slash and the rest names a section, so `#guide/hot-water` shows the guide, scrolls to `#g-hot-water` and focuses its heading. Section elements are `g-<name>`, never the fragment itself, so the browser's own jump to a matching ID never fights the router (an earlier `#week` link broke that way). Opening the page on a view other than the house starts at the top. A second press on a link to the hash already showing routes again. The back button returns to where the reader was.

**Help lines.** One muted `--step--1` sentence and at most one link into the Guide, only where people get stuck: the key under the house ("Bar on a room: radiator on." and "Reading the house"), Today, the Schedules line, the Devices head, the Suggestions line, the Savings head ("How savings are worked out") and the Needs attention panel ("What these alerts mean"). The Rooms heading has "Select a room to change it." and no link. Link text names the topic, never "Learn more" or an appended arrow. A help link (`.help-link`) is `--text`, underlined, inline-block with the usual press scale and `--cool` on hover, and gets 13 px of extra hit area on touch screens. Help lines are made once and never redrawn with the live data, so focus on a link survives each tick. No tooltips or popovers.

**Login page.** `web/login.html` and `web/login.js`, styled in `app.css`. On the page background, a column at most 440 px wide: the wordmark over a hairline, then one white panel headed "Sign in" (800, `--step-2`) with one muted line, "Sign in to see the house and change the heating." Then whichever of these the server offers: a password form (Username and Password with visible labels, `--line-strong` outlines, a primary "Sign in"), an "or" divider of `--line` hairlines, and one button per single sign-on method with the configured label (outlined beside the password form, primary when it is the only way in). Before anyone has an account, the form is replaced by a `--surface-2` band: "No one can sign in yet.", the `radiate users add` command in an outlined box, and why the page never creates the first account. Errors sit in a `--warn-soft` band in `--warn-ink` (`role="alert"`): "That username and password weren't accepted. Check them and try again.", "Too many attempts for this account. Try again in 14 min.", "Single sign-on didn't work. Try again, or ask whoever runs Radiate to check its log.", "Cannot reach Radiate. Check the connection and try again." An empty or refused field gets `aria-invalid` and an amber outline. Under the panel, a muted line says who can reset a forgotten password. The panel enters from `scale(0.98)` and `opacity: 0` like the sign-in card. The username field takes focus. Inputs are 44 px on touch screens.

**Account menu.** The last control in the header, shown only for a login session: an outlined button with the username (clipped at 16 characters) and a chevron, `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls`, filled in ink while open. It opens a panel under it, right-aligned, at most 300 px wide, with a `--line-strong` outline (it floats over the page, and there are no shadows): "Signed in as alex with a password." (or "with single sign-on"), a muted role line ("Admin: you can change anything here.", "Household: you can change temperatures, away, fires, boosts and room adjustments. Settings and the household setup need an admin." or "Read only: you can see the house but not change it."), and an outlined "Sign out". It enters from `scale(0.95)` and `opacity: 0` at the top right; under reduced motion only the fade stays. Opening it moves focus to Sign out; Escape closes it and returns focus to the button; a press outside closes it. A failed sign-out says "Not signed out. Check the connection and try again." in amber. Under 560 px it sits on the brand row after the outdoor temperature.

**Savings line.** On the House view, under today's plan, once savings have figures: the headline sentence as one help link to `#savings`. Hidden while learning or unavailable.

**Boiler panel.** A "Boiler" heading with its chip, the countdown ("Shortest burn: 3 min to go", "Can start again in 6 min") and Boost hot water; the guard bar; the last 24 hours of burns (`--heat`) and hot water (`--cool`) with ticks "24 h ago" to "Now"; then figures as label over value, not tiles: Burner today, Starts in the last hour, Burn needed now, Even heating, yesterday, and Flow to each radiator (the supply factor as a percentage). Secondary detail sits under a value in muted `--step--1`. Every value is optional: a missing key or a null shows `–`.

## Phone width

- Design for 360 px wide first. The page never scrolls sideways.
- The 16px gutter holds at every width.
- Under 1000 px the house sits above the room table and stops being sticky.
- Under 600 px the header stops being sticky, as it wraps to several lines; the Heating and Burns labels hide and the controls sit to the left, with 10 px segment padding, so both fit at 360 px with 44 px touch targets. The tabs wrap rather than scroll.
- Under 600 px the room table drops its Valve and State columns and the state sits under the room's name. The open row stacks its facts above the stepper below 640 px.
- The floor picker draws one floor on its own, which reads better on a phone than both floors at once.
- Under 520 px the names in Today narrow to 84 px.
- Long room names, readings and reasons wrap; they never push the layout wider.
- Suggestion and device cards drop to one column; long titles wrap beside their state.
- The savings chart fills its panel's width at every size; under 520 px it is 180 px tall and date labels thin out to fit. The savings table and the Raw data table scroll inside their own boxes, never the page.
- The Guide's contents sit above the text below 980 px, in as many 240 px columns as fit; the Settings contents use 150 px columns. Settings rows stack their control under the help below 640 px.
- The tabs, the editor's floor buttons and its room chips are 44 px tall on touch screens.
- The login page is one column at every width, with the 16px gutter. The account menu is at most the page width less the gutters.
- The setup wizard is at most 820 px wide. Under 560 px the step line moves above Back and Next. Room cards in the setup drop to one column below 300 px each.
